3# The outline of the area each map marker is in: its country, state or
4# province, county, city, town or neighbourhood (the level).
5#
6# A map can draw the boundary of the area around each of its markers.  The
7# outlines come from OpenStreetMap's Nominatim, through its reverse lookup:
8# given a point and a zoom, it names the administrative area there and, with
9# polygon_geojson, sends its shape.  Asking by point rather than by name means
10# the same code serves coordinate columns and geocoded places, in any country,
11# and needs no state or country column to tell one Richmond from another.
12#
13# Nominatim allows one request a second, so this module asks as little as it
14# can:
15#
16#   1. Each point is rounded to 3 decimal places (about 110 m), which is also
17#      all of it that leaves this machine, and remembered with the outline it
18#      fell in (or with none, for a point at sea).
19#   2. A point not remembered is first tested against the outlines already
20#      known, through an index of their bounding boxes, so ten thousand shops
21#      in one county cost one request, not ten thousand.
22#   3. Only then is Nominatim asked, at most online_max times a call, and a
23#      point it does not answer is left pending, not remembered as nowhere.
24#
25# The outlines are simplified by Nominatim (polygon_threshold), about 7 KB each,
26# because they are written into the page.
27#
28# What Nominatim has at each level differs by country, and was probed in
29# November 2026 (the points and what came back are in the comments at %LEVELS).
30# A level is therefore a plan: a list of tries, each a reverse zoom and the
31# place ranks an answer may have, taken in order until one gives an outline.
32#
33#   country        zoom 3, rank 4.
34#   state          zoom 5, rank 8: a US or Indian state, a Canadian province, a
35#                  New Zealand region, England or Scotland.
36#   county         below, one rank a country.
37#   city           zoom 10, ranks 12 to 16, an administrative area or a place
38#                  mapped as an area: Toronto, Manchester, Canterbury (the
39#                  district), Bristol.  Where zoom 10 gives only a point (York,
40#                  Pune), zoom 8 at rank 12: the unitary authority or city
41#                  district.  A point in no city gets its rank 12 area
42#                  too (a US county): zoom 10 answers with it.  New Zealand's cities are its territorial
43#                  authorities, rank 12 at zoom 8 (Hamilton City), or the region
44#                  where that is one (Auckland).
45#   town           zoom 12, ranks 13 to 18: Margate, a Toronto ward.  Then zoom
46#                  14, ranks 19 and 20, administrative areas only: a town
47#                  that is a civil parish (Westgate-on-Sea).
48#   neighbourhood  zoom 14, ranks 17 to 20: a Toronto neighbourhood, an English
49#                  civil parish (Westgate-on-Sea), a suburb mapped as an area
50#                  (Manchester's City Centre).  Most suburbs are mapped as a
51#                  point (Cliftonville, Garlinge), and those have no outline.
52#
53# The larger the area, the more its outline is simplified (the level's detail),
54# or a country would be megabytes.  An outline is kept under its detail, so one
55# relation drawn at two levels (Auckland, a state and a city) is kept twice.
56#
57# A "county" is the division of a country below its states.  Which
58# of Nominatim's place ranks that is depends on the country, so %LEVELS gives a
59# rank for most (12: a US county, an English county, a German Landkreis, a
60# French departement, a Pakistani district) and the countries that differ (10:
61# an Indian or Bangladeshi district, a Chinese prefecture, a Spanish province;
62# 8: a Kenyan county), and config map_bounds_ranks can add more.  Each reply
63# names its country, so a point is asked at the zoom of the last country seen,
64# and again at its own country's zoom when that guess was wrong: one extra
65# request at each border a map crosses.  The rank an outline was accepted at is
66# kept with it, so a changed table asks again rather than draw the old level.
67# The other levels do the same with their plans: a point is asked the first try
68# of the last country's plan, and its own country's plan from there.

=head1 DESCRIPTION

Finds the area each point is in, at one level, and returns its outline as a GeoJSON
Feature, for L<HTML::OSM>'s C<add_geojson>.  The outlines come from
OpenStreetMap's Nominatim reverse lookup, through the same throttled user agent
as the geocoder (L<Database::BI::Model::Geocode/nominatim_ua>), so the two
together never ask more than once a second.

Only the point is sent, rounded to three decimal places (about 110 metres).
Every answer is cached: a point with the outline it is in, and the outline
itself.  A point inside an outline already known is matched here, without a
request, so many points in one county cost one lookup.

The levels, largest first:

=over 4

=item country

=item state

A US or Indian state, a Canadian province, a New Zealand region, England.

=item county

The default; see below.

=item city

A city or the district named after it: Toronto, Manchester, the district of
Canterbury, Hamilton City in New Zealand.  Where OpenStreetMap has no city round a point, or has the city only as a point
(York, Pune), the area at the county's rank there: the unitary authority, the
city district, or the county itself.

=item town

A town or city ward: Margate, Toronto Centre.  An English town that is a civil
parish (Westgate-on-Sea) is that parish.

=item neighbourhood

A neighbourhood, suburb or civil parish that OpenStreetMap has as an area.
Many suburbs are mapped only as a point, and those have no outline.

=back

Larger areas are simplified more, so an outline is a few kilobytes at any
level.

A county here is the division of a country below its states, whose place rank
in Nominatim depends on the country: 12 (a US county or independent city, an
English county, a German Landkreis, a French departement) unless the country is
listed otherwise: 10 for India, Bangladesh, China and Spain (district,
prefecture, province), 8 for Kenya.  C<ranks> adds or changes countries.
Anything else Nominatim answers is taken as "no county here" and remembered as
such.  A country with nothing at its rank (Australia, Brazil, Japan) gets no
outlines.

Each reply names the point's country, so a point is asked at the zoom of the
last country seen, and asked again when its own country wants another rank.
An outline is kept with the rank it was accepted at; when C<ranks> changes, the
points it covered are asked again.
